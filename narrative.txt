**Section 7: Thematic Analysis and Taxonomic Alignment in the Literature Review**

The present literature review employed a mixed-methods approach to systematically identify and analyze emergent themes within the corpus of recent academic research. The methodology integrated computational topic modeling with qualitative thematic analysis, adhering to the six-phase framework proposed by Braun and Clarke (2006) while leveraging BERTopic, a state-of-the-art natural language processing (NLP) technique, to enhance thematic discovery. Initially, BERTopic was applied to the abstracts of 60 peer-reviewed articles to generate preliminary topic clusters based on semantic similarity. This computational phase involved document embedding using pre-trained language models, dimensionality reduction via UMAP, and clustering through HDBSCAN, which collectively enabled the identification of latent thematic structures within the text. Subsequently, the Braun and Clarke (2006) framework guided the manual refinement of these clusters through iterative coding, theme development, and review. This hybrid approach ensured both the scalability of computational methods and the interpretive rigor of qualitative analysis, thereby mitigating the limitations inherent in purely automated or manual thematic extraction.

The analysis revealed eight distinct themes, each representing a cohesive strand of contemporary scholarly inquiry. *Digital Transformation and Innovation* emerged as the most prominent theme, encompassing research on the adoption, impacts, and challenges of digital technologies across industries, including generative AI, big data analytics, and supply chain digitalization. *Supply Chain Management and Resilience* focused on strategies for mitigating disruptions, such as blockchain implementation and supplier clustering algorithms, while *Technology Adoption and User Behavior* explored the psychological and organizational barriers to technological change, including user resistance and technostress. *Research Methodologies and Analytical Frameworks* highlighted the diverse approaches underpinning empirical studies, from structural equation modeling to multi-criteria decision-making methods. *Organizational Performance and Management* addressed firm-level outcomes, including cybersecurity culture and IT outsourcing conflicts, whereas *Healthcare Technology and Systems* examined the intersection of digital tools and medical applications, such as health data security and wearable adoption. *Emerging Technologies and Applications* captured cutting-edge developments, including pre-trained models and non-fungible token (NFT) pricing, while *Sustainability and Circular Economy* underscored the role of digital innovation in environmental sustainability, particularly through IoT and waste-to-energy solutions.

To contextualize these findings within the broader field of information systems research, the identified themes were mapped onto the *Pacific Asia Journal of the Association for Information Systems* (PAJAIS) taxonomy. Six of the eight themes aligned closely with existing PAJAIS categories, demonstrating their established relevance within the discipline. *Digital Transformation and Innovation* and *Organizational Performance and Management* were classified under *Business Intelligence* (match confidence: 0.8 and 0.7, respectively), reflecting their focus on data-driven decision-making and organizational outcomes. *Supply Chain Management and Resilience* corresponded to *Decision Support Systems* (0.85), given its emphasis on optimization and disruption mitigation, while *Technology Adoption and User Behavior* mapped to *Human-Computer Interaction* (0.75), highlighting its behavioral and user-centric dimensions. *Healthcare Technology and Systems* exhibited a near-perfect alignment with *Healthcare & Biomedical AI* (0.95), and *Emerging Technologies and Applications* was categorized under *Artificial Intelligence Methods* (0.8). However, two themes—*Research Methodologies and Analytical Frameworks* and *Sustainability and Circular Economy*—were deemed *novel* within the PAJAIS framework, as they do not correspond to any single predefined category. This novelty suggests potential gaps in the taxonomy’s coverage of methodological advancements and sustainability-focused research, warranting further exploration.

Despite the strengths of this computational approach, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the reliance on abstracts rather than full-text analysis may have excluded nuanced thematic details embedded in the body of the articles. Second, while BERTopic excels at identifying semantic clusters, it may overlook thematic subtleties that require human interpretive judgment, particularly in cases of polysemy or domain-specific jargon. Third, the PAJAIS mapping, though systematic, is inherently subjective, as it relies on the researchers’ interpretation of taxonomic boundaries. Additionally, the hybrid methodology, while rigorous, is time-intensive, limiting its scalability for larger corpora. Finally, the thematic analysis reflects only the most salient trends within the sampled literature, potentially overlooking niche or nascent research areas that have yet to gain prominence.

The implications of these findings for future research are multifaceted. The identification of novel themes—particularly *Sustainability and Circular Economy*—highlights an opportunity to expand the PAJAIS taxonomy to better accommodate interdisciplinary and emerging research domains. Future studies could employ full-text analysis or incorporate citation networks to refine thematic discovery, while mixed-methods approaches could further validate computational findings through expert interviews or Delphi panels. Moreover, the prominence of *Digital Transformation and Innovation* underscores the need for longitudinal studies to track the evolution of digital technologies and their organizational impacts. Similarly, the methodological focus of *Research Methodologies and Analytical Frameworks* suggests a growing interest in innovative research designs, which could inform best practices for empirical inquiry in information systems. Ultimately, this analysis not only synthesizes current scholarly trends but also provides a roadmap for advancing both thematic and taxonomic frameworks in the field.
